# Source passages and later result checks

Consolidated from source inspections on 22 October 2026; the two principal papers were reopened and their relevant passages rechecked while preparing this manuscript. The research target uses strictly positive additive values, complete allocations, exact EFX, and Pareto comparisons against every complete integral allocation. A source concerning fractional efficiency, balanced competitors, MMS, chores, approximate EFX, or an additional ex-ante fairness requirement does not by itself resolve that target.

| Source and inspected passage | What it supports | Scope for this question |
|---|---|---|
| Jin and Tao, [On Pareto-Optimal and Fair Allocations with Personalized Bi-Valued Utilities, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.18251v2), abstract, §1.1 and §5 | EFX exists for the specified positive personalized bivalued goods model. §5 explicitly asks about EFX together with PO and separates two failed routes: ordinary maximum Nash welfare can violate EFX; EFX can conflict with fractional PO. | This is the exact open problem motivating the work. Its fractional counterexample does not rule out integral PO. |
| Lin, Wu and Zhou, [When Maximum Nash Welfare Becomes Strongly Fair, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00122v1), abstract, Table 1, §3.1 and conclusion | The personalized bivalued MNW EFX guarantee is 2/3, with tightness addressed in the paper. | This concerns fairness of global MNW, not maximization restricted to EFX and not a general exact EFX+PO existence theorem. |
| Byrka, Malinka and Ponitka, [Probing EFX via PMMS, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.14957v2), abstract and personalized bivalued section | Another EFX existence result; stronger PMMS under an integral divisibility condition. | No global integral PO guarantee for arbitrary personalized ratios was established by the inspected passages. |
| Cookson, Shah and Verma, [Fair and Efficient Balanced Allocations for Additive Valuations, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06325v1), abstract, Theorem 1 and Lemma 1 | Fairness and efficiency results with balanced allocations and the relevant balanced fractional comparison domain. | Restricting the comparison domain to balanced allocations is material. These statements cannot be substituted for unrestricted global PO or exact EFX. |
| Zhang, [Exact MMS Allocations under Personalized Bivalued Valuations, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15822v1), abstract, introduction and technical overview; full-page search for Pareto | The paper claims exact MMS existence and polynomial computation for personalized bivalued goods and chores. | MMS is a different guarantee. The inspected text supplies no joint EFX+PO theorem; this audit does not independently certify its MMS proof. |
| Aziz, [Best-of-Both-Worlds Fairness and Pareto Optimality, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08966v1), abstract, main results, definitions and §6 Theorem 5 | A two-agent lottery result with EFX+PO outcomes, and a three-agent/four-good impossibility when ex-ante envy-freeness is additionally required. | The latter rules out a suitable lottery, not all deterministic EFX+PO allocations. Its example is not personalized bivalued in every row. |
| Amanatidis, Birmpas, Filos-Ratsikas, Hollender and Voudouris, [Maximum Nash Welfare and Other Stories About EFX](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/0004.pdf), abstract, contribution summary and definition of a two-value instance | For a common pair of values, maximum Nash welfare yields EFX. The common-pair domain is explicit. | This is useful background, but agent-dependent ratios are a larger domain. No new common-pair existence claim is made here. |

Earlier source checks distinguished zero-valued welfare-complexity results and chores impossibility from this strictly positive goods question. Those observations are background, not new full-paper audits.

The search included combinations of “personalized bi-valued,” “EFX,” “Pareto-optimal,” “2026,” and Nash maximization within the EFX set. Primary sources were opened before substantive reliance. Search aggregators were used only to locate papers. A publisher page for *Fair Division of Indivisible Items: Envy-Freeness vs. Efficiency Revisited* returned HTTP 429; its contents are not used as evidence. The inspected sources contain no resolution of the unrestricted target. This is a scoped literature check, not an exhaustive novelty review.

The new residual-quota theorem is an extension of the local v7 maximal-sink argument. The phase characterization and finite-pool geometry build on the earlier exact integer-compensation criterion, restated and proved in their v8 files.
