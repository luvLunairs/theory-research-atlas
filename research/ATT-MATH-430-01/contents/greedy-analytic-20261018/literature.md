# Sources and the scope of the current search

Checked on 18 October 2026. The target is the strict two-positive-seed
construction studied by Daan van Berkel and Wieb Bosma, not the variant allowing
equal summands. Their [Periodicity conjectures for all 2-sumfree sequences,
arXiv:2609.18522v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18522v1) was reopened in full
text. Conjecture 1 is the eventual-periodicity target; Definition 3 and
the subsequent predictions motivate the parameter split. Theorems 12
and 14 supply already known near-diagonal and diagonal cases. The full
statement and proof of the displayed Theorem 15 were also reopened;
some internal references call it Corollary 15. The new analytic replacements
use global set identities or extinction, so that finite-continuation
theorem is not a premise for them.

The same authors' [On t-sumfree sequences,
arXiv:2609.16843v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16843v1) was reopened.
Its Section 5, Theorems 9 and 10 and Remarks 12 already cover every
first-seed-one case. These remain attributed known results, not a new
contribution of this draft. The first paper's Example 13 describes
the exceptional diagonal S(2,4).

The current queries included `"2609.18522" proof periodicity` and
`"2-sumfree" "periodicity" proof`. No later primary proof was identified.
Search results are discovery aids; no secondary summary is a mathematical
premise. This bounded search does not establish that no later proof exists.
The [previous source record](../greedy-proof-audit-20261018/literature.md)
retains the inspected source-history and earlier historical checks.
Full-text gaps for Queneau, Finch's 1992 work and relevant Guy passages
remain. Consequently historical novelty, priority and significance are
not established by this attempt.

The previous local formulas, proofs, failed extensions, code and attribution
remain in ten immutable snapshots. New files cite the exact inherited
mathematical appendices they use. A prior certificate suggests a formula;

diagnostics are credited and retained separately. The shared assistance
note covers all same-team AI mathematical input and the required manual
submission route. No person has been named as a reviewer or collaborator
without an actual agreed role.
