# Quadratic generation of matroid toric ideals: the weak White conjecture

[Browse](../README.md) · [Results](../results.md) · [Contribute](../../CONTRIBUTING.md)

Ask whether every algebraic relation among matroid bases can be built from relations involving just two bases at a time. Recent counterexamples to stronger statements make the exact choice of permitted moves essential.

## The task

### Precise statement

For every finite matroid M of positive rank on [n], with set of bases B(M), let phi: Q[y_B : B in B(M)] -&gt; Q[x_1,...,x_n] send y_B to the product of x_i for i in B. Is ker(phi) generated by homogeneous quadratic binomials? All quadratic binomials preserving element multiplicities are allowed; the claim does not require symmetric-exchange generators or a quadratic Gröbner basis.

**Required output:** A proof for all finite matroids under this formulation, a fully verified counterexample, or a clearly delimited intermediate result whose novelty is separately checked.

**Scope:** The weak quadratic-generation version for arbitrary finite matroids. It is not bounded by rank or ground-set size and is not classified as a cheap exhaustive-search task.

### Assumptions

- M is a finite matroid of positive rank on the labeled ground set [n]; loops and parallel elements are allowed.
- There is one indeterminate y_B for each distinct basis; the coefficient field in this card is Q.
- A permitted quadratic replaces two bases A,B by two bases C,D whenever their element-count vectors agree, with repetitions allowed.
- The question is generation as an ideal; it imposes no term order and no requirement that each move be a single symmetric exchange.

### Success criteria

1. For a positive resolution, prove ideal generation for every finite matroid, not merely for examined instances.
2. For a negative resolution, specify a matroid by verifiable data and give a binomial in ker(phi) together with a certificate that it is not in the ideal generated by all allowed quadrics.
3. Alternatively, a finite disconnected fiber under all two-basis replacements can support a counterexample, provided the algebraic equivalence and completeness of the move graph are established.
4. Any partial result must name its matroid class, rank or degree restrictions and remain separate from the unrestricted status.

### Background needed

- Matroid axioms, bases and representability
- Toric ideals and binomial ideal membership
- Fibers of monomial maps
- Exact algebraic computation and certificate design

## Resources and verification

### Resource requirements

**Computing:** No general feasible resource bound is asserted. Basis sets, high-degree fibers and ideal-membership calculations can grow rapidly. Any future finite search must first declare rank, ground-set size, fiber degree, representation and stopping limits.

**Data:** For computational work, explicit matroid descriptions and complete basis lists or certified independence oracles. Any external database needs a version, provenance and checks of its represented class.

**Experiments:** No physical experiment; mathematical objects and exact certificates are required.

**Theory and model requirements:** Matroid axioms over a finite ground set and the declared polynomial-ring map over Q. The field used to represent a matroid, when applicable, is distinct from the coefficient field of its toric ideal.

**Validation modes:** Proof, Exact computation.

### Verification steps

1. Refresh the literature status and identify the exact weak version before starting a new attempt.
2. Verify the matroid axioms and the completeness of the basis representation.
3. Check that every quadratic preserving multiplicities is admitted in the relevant fiber graph or ideal.
4. Independently check the proposed algebraic proof or finite certificate and the bridge between its encoding and ker(phi).
5. Ask a matroid or toric-ideal specialist to assess correctness and a separate literature review to assess novelty.

**Statement alignment:** The source's arbitrary two-basis replacements match the weak ideal-generation claim. Symmetric-exchange disconnection and absence of a quadratic Gröbner basis alone do not answer this card.

**Human review difficulty:** High: both combinatorial and commutative-algebra expertise may be needed, especially for computational nonmembership claims.

### Formal validity and empirical support

**What the formal task can establish:** Any answer must concern the exact ring map and all quadratic binomials allowed by it, with an independently checked proof or certificate.

**Empirical support and limits:** No empirical claim is involved; finite computations can provide exact evidence only for their fully specified finite scope.

### Methods and obstacles

**Proposed methods:**

- Compare equivalent fiber and ideal formulations
- Exact enumeration under explicitly declared limits
- Binomial ideal-membership certificates
- Investigate selected matroid classes and structural reductions

**Expected obstacles:**

- The same name is used for inequivalent conjectures, including variants already reported false.
- Enumerating only symmetric exchanges is insufficient for this question.
- Testing one degree or finitely many matroids cannot prove the universal statement.
- A numerical or heuristic solver report is not a certificate of ideal nonmembership.

### Checks still needed

- A current specialist literature review is still needed before committing to an unrestricted attempt; no exhaustive proof of open status is possible from this check.
- Original White (1980) and the cited results on special classes have not been directly inspected here.
- Recent preprints and their computational artifacts need independent review before their stronger-variant results are treated as settled atlas facts.

**Research value:** The question links combinatorial exchange structure to the algebra of matroid base configurations. A resolution would clarify what survives after stronger exchange and Gröbner-basis claims fail.
