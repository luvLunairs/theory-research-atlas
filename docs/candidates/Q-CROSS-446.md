Research direction: connect rich sensory representations to models that retain task-relevant information over a delay and predict subsequent behavior. The 2016 review proposes combining sensory models with recurrent working-memory models; a concrete task must specify stimuli, delay conditions, state dynamics and behavioral or neural tests. This is not a single solved-or-unsolved theorem.

**Origin:** Literature-origin question. **Curator-designed scope:** No.

**Current status:** Unverified. **Last checked:** 2026-11-06.

**Status basis:** The 2016 item is a modeling program. Bates et al. provide a later model connecting deep sensory features to visual-memory errors, while distinguishing predictive fits from a more direct neural test. This is relevant partial progress, not a complete identification of working-memory mechanisms.

**Statement check:** Needs clarification. **Status check:** Later literature checked.

**Source date and freshness:** 2016 sensory-cortex review; published 2024 visual working-memory model.

**Scope limits:** The source proposes a sensory-memory interface rather than a universal recurrence theorem. Behavioral prediction, fitted state dynamics and identification of biological mechanisms are separate claims.

**Information still needed**

- Select a delay task, sensory representation, state model and out-of-sample behavioral or neural criterion.
- A complete 2024-2026 review and novelty assessment of any proposed subproblem remain pending.

**Primary branch:** [Theoretical neuroscience](../domains/cog.md#cog-neuro). **Related branches:** None recorded.

**Sources:** [Eight open questions in the computational modeling of higher sensory cortex](../sources.md#src-cross-404); [Scaling models of visual working memory to natural images](../sources.md#src-model-803).

**Exact locator:** Yamins and DiCarlo (2016), How should working memory be included?, p. 118; Bates, Alvarez and Gershman (2024), Discussion and Data/Code availability.

**Potential difficulty:** High. The target involves model choice, competing mechanisms or extrapolation; a narrow instance may be manageable, but the full research direction has no established cost bound.

**Possible AI role:** Compare a selected sensory-memory model with the published feature-based behavioral baseline, then make its delay-task and validation target explicit.

**Verification burden:** High. Check formal deductions and numerical convergence separately from empirical fit, identifiability and causal interpretation. A good fit alone cannot identify the mechanism.

**Resource expectations:** The 2024 paper links human behavioral data, analysis code and a Zenodo release. Reuse may support a bounded modeling study without new experiments, but licenses, runtime and replication were not audited. Neural-mechanism claims may require further recordings.

**Assessment:** 2026-11-06; untested forecast. **Uncertainty:** A useful published baseline exists; it does not turn the broad 2016 program into a uniquely specified open problem. No project model evaluation or empirical replication was run.

**Further work:** resolve the statement and status questions above before planning an attempt. **Human review:** Pending.
