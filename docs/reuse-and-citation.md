# Reuse and citation

Original atlas text and curated data may be copied, adapted and redistributed under CC BY 4.0; code is available under MIT. The [license statement](../LICENSE.md) defines scope and third-party exceptions. Reuse within these terms requires no additional project permission.

## What to credit

| Material used | Credit |
| --- | --- |
| Statement, theorem or method | Original research authors and the supporting work or passage |
| Card, source map or curated selection | Theory Research Atlas, stable entry ID, relevant contributors and consulted version |
| Proof, construction, counterexample or program | Actual authors, research record and code or data version |
| Idea, proof sketch or proposed method | Originator, relevant prior work and AI assistance |
| Review | Reviewer, precise review scope and recorded attribution preference |

Cite the original literature for scientific content and the atlas for its summaries, organization or research records. A problem's proposer, curator, solver and reviewer may be different people. Do not substitute the founder's name for a research author's name.

## Acknowledge AI assistance

Every submitted proof and research idea, including incomplete or unsuccessful approaches, must credit its sources and AI assistance. The [AI contribution policy](ai-contributions.md) distinguishes scientific contributions from maintenance and source curation. For maintenance or curation, a short note naming the assistance, tool or model if known, and actual checks is sufficient; scientific model eligibility and submission screening do not apply.

For scientific work, disclose assistance with the argument, evidence, research code or verdict, including contributions incorporated from other agents. Use this acknowledgment, replacing the placeholders with actual details:

> AI assistance: [provider and exact displayed model name], [version or unavailable], [configuration], used on [date] for [role and affected sections]. Research inputs and outputs: [versioned references or explanation of unavailable material]. Human changes and checks: [description and contributor].

Identify every contributing model; a linked, versioned assistance note can hold a longer record. State unavailable details explicitly rather than inferring them from product names. Continue to cite the papers, code and data underlying the argument, and open sources suggested by a model before citing them. An acknowledgment records provenance, not correctness. Models are tools, not human authors or reviewers; submission screening does not establish independent scientific review.

## Suggested citations

For the collection, use [CITATION.cff](../CITATION.cff) and identify the revision consulted:

> luvLunairs and Theory Research Atlas contributors. *Theory Research Atlas*. Version 0.1.0-dev. Dataset. Include the revision or snapshot date consulted.

For an entry:

> Entry contributors. “Entry title.” *Theory Research Atlas*, problem ID, revision or version, date accessed. Entry URL.

Use named entry contributors where recorded, otherwise **luvLunairs and Theory Research Atlas contributors**. [AUTHORS.md](../AUTHORS.md) distinguishes project responsibilities from research authorship. Keep the stable problem ID after title or classification changes. If no stable URL exists, provide the repository-relative path and revision. Do not invent an archive identifier or DOI.

Cite the version actually consulted; an atlas access date does not update a mathematical claim's date. For a research result, prefer its own citation and named contributors, linking the attempt or review and revision. Preserve its status as candidate, partial or reviewed within a specified scope. A reproduction credits both the original discovery and the people who repeated it; an unreviewed record must not be called peer reviewed.

## Reusing and modifying material

For **CC BY** material, retain author credit and relevant notices, link to the license and source where reasonably practicable, identify your modifications and preserve earlier modification notices. Place attribution with the material or in a clearly linked credits section. For **MIT** code, retain the copyright and license notice in copies or substantial portions.

Academic citations and accurate role descriptions complement these legal duties; they do not add restrictions to the standard licenses.

Check external resources' rights separately. A link, summary or citation does not grant permission to redistribute a complete paper, figure, database or program. Record the source and license of third-party material you include. Use an original summary and link if redistribution rights are unclear.

Research evidence needs a retained version as well as permission to share it. Follow [evidence preservation](maintaining.md#preserve-the-evidence) for artifact manifests, local checksums, external verification limits and version changes. Keep earlier evidence and reviews available when correcting a result; replacing a proof or program does not preserve the applicability of its earlier review.

## Credit and corrections

Use people's stated names or handles and credit only their actual work. Do not infer identities, invent affiliations or list approving reviewers without their agreement. Submitters remain responsible for accuracy, provenance and rights, including disclosed AI assistance.

Keep corrections and retractions with the affected entry or result. Propose attribution corrections through the [contribution channel](../CONTRIBUTING.md) with supporting evidence. For suspected infringement or permission concerns, use the [rights reporting process](../LICENSE.md#rights-concerns). Retain conflicting priority claims and their sources until resolved.
